# Do not claim

Things that are false, or true enough to be dangerous, about MySaveFile.

This file exists because an LLM given only positive facts extrapolates
cheerfully and in the product's own voice. Everything below is a claim the
surface files rule out — each one paired with what is actually true, so the
correction is usable rather than just a prohibition.

**Read this before writing anything public**: a landing page, a guide, a store
listing, a social post, a reply to a user. Every entry here is one somebody has
either already made or would reasonably assume.

---

## 0 · Words this product does not use

**✗ "town", "your town", "the whole town".** The Sims 4 is not set in a town.
A save holds **worlds**; a world holds **neighbourhoods** and **lots**. Reach for
**your save**, **your plan**, **a world**, or just say who or what you mean.

**✓ "townie" is fine**, because it is the game's own word for a sim the game
generated. So is **Rest of Town**, which is a label on the households screen and
part of that screen's locked vocabulary — as is **Town at a glance**, the
heading on its third column. The ban is on the loose noun, not on the two places
the product already says it deliberately.

Why this is worth a rule: marketing writes from these files, so a word that
drifts in here becomes a public claim in the game's own players' language, and
they will notice. It has been swept once already (2026-08-26, sixteen instances
across ten sheets).

## 1 · The game, and your save file

**✗ "It edits your save."** · **✗ "Apply your plan to your game."** ·
**✗ "One click and it's in the game."**

**✓** Nothing the planner does ever reaches your `.save` file or your game. The
file is read; it is never written. Everything you author here — a club, a
holiday, a business, a venue, a dynasty, a sim's career — is **a plan you still
have to carry out in-game yourself**. Every manager says so in its own words:
*a plan, not a save edit*.

**✗ "Upload your save."** · **✗ "We scan your saves in the cloud."**

**✓** The file is read **locally in your browser**. Only the extracted records —
lots, sims, households and the rest — are sent to the server. The import screen
says this while you're deciding, and it needs to stay true.

**✗ "It reads your Mods folder."** · **✗ "It detects the packs you have
installed."**

**✓** Nothing on your computer is read except the one `.save` file you pick.
Your mods list is typed by hand. Pack ownership is **inferred from evidence
inside a save**, not from your installation.

**✗ "Sync your plan back to the game."**

**✓** Sync runs one way only: game → plan. There is no other direction, and no
part of the product has ever had one.

## 2 · Re-sync

**✗ "Undo a sync."** · **✗ "Roll back."**

**✓** There is no undo. What exists is a **full backup taken before every sync**,
parked in Deleted & backups for **7 days**, which you restore as a save. If the
backup can't be made, the sync stops rather than proceeding without one.

**✗ "Choose which changes to accept."** · **✗ "Review and approve each
change."**

**✓** A sync applies **wholesale**. The review screen is a count and a button —
nothing is selectable, because you'd be making those choices on every sync
forever.

**✗ "See what changed last time."** · **✗ "Sync history."**

**✓** Nothing is written down. Each record stores only what the save last said
about it, so once a sync lands the previous values are gone. A count tells you
*four holidays are different*, never which four fields moved.

**✗ "Nothing changed."**

**✓** Never say this, even when the screen shows no counts. Skills, careers,
funds, causes of death and family relationships refresh on **every** sync
without ever reaching a count. The screen's own wording is *No new households,
sims or lots* for exactly this reason.

**✗ "Your edits are always safe."**

**✓** Precisely: an edit you made that the game did not touch survives. Where
the game changed the same thing, **the game wins** — that is the point of a
plan that stays true. And where a plan becomes impossible (a business on a lot a
family moved onto, a club hangout the game rezoned), the plan **yields**,
quietly.

## 3 · Packs

**✗ "It knows which packs you own."** · **✗ "Automatic pack detection."**

**✓** It infers, from what a save happens to contain, and it is honest about
being a floor rather than a census. A pack you own but haven't used in the save
you synced stays off until you switch it on. Packs with no world of their own are
the weak cases — Tiny Living's only evidence is a tiny-home lot, and there isn't
one in any of the 71 saves in the development database.

**✗ "Content you don't own is hidden / blocked / locked away."**

**✓** Locking is **soft everywhere**. A gated page still opens and works in full.
A locked world still opens. Anything already in your plan from a pack you don't
own keeps showing. Ownership gates what you can **add**, never what exists.

**✗ "Set which packs each save uses."**

**✓** Pack ownership is **one setting for your whole account**. Switching a pack
off changes every save you have, and syncing one save changes what every save
offers.

**✗ "Every Sims 4 pack is supported."**

**✓** 49 packs are tracked — the ones that gate something in the planner. Kits
that only add objects, hair and clothing aren't in the list at all.

## 4 · Phones

**✗ "Plan on the go."** · **✗ "Works everywhere."** · **✗ "Mobile app."**

**✓** Nine screens are **desktop-only** by design: households, sims, clubs,
holidays, small businesses, custom venues, dynasties, mods and the family tree.
Importing and syncing are desktop-only too, because the `.save` lives on the
computer with the game on it. What does work on a phone: Home, world pages, the
lot editor, inspo, the randomizer, Diversity, pack settings, save management and
every account screen.

## 5 · What's public, and what isn't

**✗ "Share your town."** — for two reasons. It doesn't say which half, and
**"town" is not a word this product uses**; see §0.

**✓** A public `/share/…` link serves the **showcase** only: showcase photos,
descriptions, lots and worlds. Everything else is private and appears on no
public page — **inspo photos, clubs, holidays, small businesses, dynasties,
custom venues, mods, notes, and every sim's private notes**.

**✗ "Notes"** and **"descriptions"** used interchangeably.

**✓** **Notes are private, always.** **Descriptions are public** — they show on a
shared showcase. Two different fields on the same records.

**✗ "Link to your Sims Gallery profile."**

**✓** EA publishes no creator profile page. The Gallery ID is a **search handle**
you type into the game, and it renders as plain `@name` text. It has never been a
link and must not be described as one.

## 6 · Accounts

**✗ "Delete your account."** · **✗ "Change your email."** · **✗ "Sign out
everywhere."** · **✗ "Two-factor authentication."** · **✗ "Recovery codes."**

**✓** None of these exist. Saves can be deleted one at a time; the account behind
them can't. The email address is shown and not editable. Signing out clears the
current browser only. **The email address on the account is the only way back
in.**

**✗ "Stay signed in."**

**✓** Seven days, fixed, counted from when you signed in — using the app daily
does not extend it.

## 7 · What the planner can't see or say

**✗ "Every lot in the game."**

**✓** 402 lots are seeded into every save; **eight are hidden on purpose** —
Sylvan Glade, the Forgotten Grotto, the Magic Realm, Sixam, the hermit's hut, the
acting studio, FutureSim Labs and the Netherworld — because there's nothing to
plan on them.

**✗ "Every club activity / icon / name."**

**✓** 47 of the game's 278 club activities have no art and can't be picked. Icon
libraries are finite (26 club, 30 holiday, 30 business), and anything outside
them falls back to a question mark. A stock club whose name isn't on the
planner's list of eleven reads **Stock Club**.

**✗ "It names your mods' content."**

**✓** A modded activity's name lives in the mod's own package file, which the
save doesn't carry. It draws a puzzle piece — that is the whole of what can
honestly be said about it. Role outfits are the same: stored as numbers with no
names attached.

**✗ "Plan a sim to level 10."**

**✓** Levels are progress, and progress only exists in the save. You can plan
**which** skills and **which** career; you cannot plan a level, and anything
ranked by level counts built skills only.

**✗ "Build a family tree."**

**✓** The tree is read out of your save. Authoring goes **upward only** — name an
Unknown, invent a parent, record a bond. There is no add-child, no add-partner,
no delete, and it's humans only. A plan with no imported save has no tree at
all, and no way to start one. A household you invented never becomes a family
line.

**✗ "Never lose an ancestor."**

**✓** True only from the point you started planning. A save written before the
game's February 2026 family-tree patch carries no premade ancestors at all, and
downloaded saves are often in that state.

## 8 · Editing

**✗ "Edit anything, anywhere."**

**✓** Records that came from your save are **read-only, with Notes as the
exception** — clubs, holidays, businesses, dynasties, venues, and every field on
an imported sim except the ones the planner treats as goals. Sims are edited in
**Households** and nowhere else; the sim roster edits notes and a portrait only.

**✗ "Delete a sim / household / club from your save."**

**✓** Deleting removes it from **the plan**. The save is untouched, and for
anything the save still holds, the next sync brings it straight back — without
the notes you'd written on it. Only plan-only records delete for good.

**✗ "Re-age a sim."** · **✗ "Un-earn a degree."** · **✗ "Move a sim you
invented."**

**✓** Lifestage is read-only on a sim from your save (re-aging would cascade
through traits, skills and career); degrees from the save are locked; only sims
that exist in the save can carry a planned move.

## 9 · Numbers and identity

**✗ Quoting any figure in this documentation as a product claim.**

**✓** Counts in the surface files come from one developer's saves — *209
households*, *406 sims*, *78 families*, *22 clubs*. They illustrate what a real
save holds; they are not typical, guaranteed, or a benchmark.

**✗ Anything implying EA involvement** — "official", "partnered with",
"approved", "powered by EA", or using EA's marks as our own.

**✓** *The Sims™ is a trademark of Electronic Arts Inc. This is an unofficial,
non-commercial fan project, not affiliated with or endorsed by EA.* That line is
in the app's own sidebar and the position it states is the one to hold.

**✗ "CAS"** for anything we built.

**✓** CAS is the game's own creator. Ours is the **creation modal** or the **sim
builder**.
